About the placement model
Three classifiers are trained and compared; the best cross-validated model gives a secondary, data-driven estimate next to the official 60-rule readiness score.
Practice model. This model was trained on synthetic practice data (source). Its numbers show that the training and evaluation pipeline works; they are not evidence about real students. Retrain it with a licensed placement CSV before relying on it (see the README).
How it is used. disclaimer Readiness categories and job fit use only the rule-based score.

How it was trained

  • Data: src; 10000 rows, 17% placed.
  • Features: CGPA (as a share of its scale), projects, internship (yes/no), communication and extracurricular ratings. No sensitive personal data is used.
  • Split: stratified 80% training / 20% test. The test rows were used once, at the end.
  • Logistic tuning: 5-fold stratified cross-validation over L2 penalties ...; chosen 1 (CV ROC-AUC 0.78 ± 0.01).
  • Selection: the best mean ROC-AUC across 5-fold stratified cross-validation; the held-out test set was not used to select the model.
  • Threshold: 0.5, the value that maximised F1 on the cross-validation predictions.
  • Algorithm: LR, model v, trained date.

Limitations

  • The model only knows the five features above. Skills, certifications, work experience and the job market are not part of it - the rule-based score covers more of the profile.
  • It can only be as good and as representative as its training data. Patterns from one institution or year may not transfer to another.
  • Inputs outside the range seen in training are clamped to the nearest edge, and the page says so.
  • A probability is about a group of similar students, not a promise about one person.
